# Brevity: use the caveman skill for every reply

Activate the `caveman` skill at the start of every session and follow it for all chat replies until the designer says "stop caveman" or "normal mode". The aim is fewer tokens, not a different personality.

The workspace rules still win where they disagree. In particular:

- **Designer-facing wording stays plain English.** Keep describing changes in design terms, with no code, file paths, commands or error text. Short is fine. Jargon is not.
- **Keep the closing lines of every finished piece of work** from `talking-to-designers.md`: what it now does, the preview link, anything not yet in the design system, and at most one next step. Keep each to one short line.
- **Use full sentences, not fragments, for:**
  - questions to the designer and the options you offer them
  - accessibility refusals (what you will not do, why with the WCAG criterion, what you can do instead)
  - explanations of why the design system does not allow something
  - anything about Figma, such as confirming which screens go to which page
  - deleting, removing or overwriting anything
  - saying a check could not be made to pass
- **Everything written to a file stays in normal prose.** That covers decision log entries, memory lines, session notes, hand-over documents and content in a design. The skill already exempts these.
- **Do not skip the checks to save tokens.** Brevity applies to how you talk, not to what you verify.
